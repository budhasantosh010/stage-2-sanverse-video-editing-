# Macro Goal

## Goal statement

Take a cleaned talking-head video plus natural multimodal user intent—chat, pointing, drawing, and simple direct manipulation—and produce an editable, verified, polished export in minutes, without requiring the user to learn a professional video editor.

## Target user

The first target user is the owner: a non-editor who already creates talking-head YouTube videos and does not want to spend hours learning or operating a complex editing application.

## Desired experience

1. Upload or select a cleaned Stage 1 video.
2. Watch or scrub to the relevant moment.
3. Point, draw, select, or write: “add this here,” “cut this,” or “make this move like this.”
4. Receive a concrete proposed change and a real preview.
5. Accept, revise, or undo it.
6. Export a polished result in minutes.

## Success is not feature parity

The macro goal is not to reproduce every panel and control from DaVinci Resolve, Premiere Pro, CapCut, and After Effects. Their underlying editing capabilities will be introduced as reusable primitives only when they enable validated user outcomes.

## Accuracy contract

“100% or near 100%” is a direction, not an unsupported metric.

- Deterministic functions such as applying a validated cut at an exact timestamp can be tested for exactness.
- Semantic interpretation such as understanding a vague sketch cannot honestly be guaranteed at 100%.
- When semantic confidence is insufficient, the system must clarify, preview, or refuse rather than silently guess.
- Reliability is measured on real tasks with explicit acceptance criteria, not by model confidence alone.

## Completion definition

The macro goal is complete only after representative non-editor workflows reliably produce acceptable finished videos within measured time and quality budgets. A code-complete application without that user evidence is not the final outcome.
